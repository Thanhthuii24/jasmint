## Background

Non-destructive testing (NDT) with pulsed eddy currents (PEC) allows us to detect subsurface defects in conductive materials without damaging them. The challenge: reconstructing a high-quality defect image from noisy PEC signals is an ill-posed inverse problem.

## Why diffusion models?

Diffusion models have shown remarkable success in image generation and restoration tasks. Their ability to model complex data distributions makes them well-suited for inverse problems.

## The physics-guided approach

Instead of treating reconstruction as a pure learning problem, we incorporate physics priors:

1. **Forward model**: simulate what a defect of shape X would produce as a PEC signal
2. **Guidance**: use this physics model to steer the diffusion sampling process
3. **Result**: reconstructions that are both data-consistent and physically plausible

## Key results

Our approach outperforms baseline deep learning methods on reconstruction quality metrics while being more interpretable — the physics guidance ensures the model doesn't hallucinate physically impossible defect shapes.
